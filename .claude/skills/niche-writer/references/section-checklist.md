# Niche Entry Self-Check

Run through this list before declaring a niche entry done. Answer each question honestly. If any answer is no or unsure, revise.

## Frontmatter

- [ ] `slug` is short, lowercase, underscore-separated, and stable.
- [ ] `display_name` is the customer-facing label that will appear in onboarding.
- [ ] `tenant_type_fit` is the right array — `[seller]`, `[doer]`, or `[seller, doer]` — based on what the business actually does.
- [ ] `aliases` includes the alternative names a maker in this category might actually use when picking from the onboarding list.
- [ ] `related_niches` lists adjacent categories the maker might grow into, not arbitrary near-matches.
- [ ] `status` is `draft`. (Approval happens after human review, not in the skill.)

## Structure

- [ ] All ten body sections are present, in order, with the exact section names: What this business does / Brand exemplars across the range / Who their customers are / How they talk about their products / Common specializations and variations / What customers ask before buying / Visual direction range / What tends to surface on the storefront / What to avoid / Adjacent niches.
- [ ] No invented sections, no rearranged sections, no abbreviated section names.

## Bias-avoidance

- [ ] No sentences start with "Most <niche> makers are…", "The typical <niche> buyer is…", "The genre has converged on…", or "Successful brands all…".
- [ ] Buyer types are plural archetypes (self, gift, functional, collector, subcultural, etc.), not one dominant frame.
- [ ] The brand-exemplar section names at least three distinct positionings — not three brands in the same direction.
- [ ] At least one of the exemplars sits outside the obvious premium-D2C cluster (devotional, folk/vintage, occult/ritual, subcultural, community-rooted, etc.).
- [ ] Visual direction is described as a range of sensibilities, with explicit acknowledgment that the mood pick and inspiration URLs choose among them.
- [ ] Any stylistic guidance is framed as a tendency the mood pick overrides, not as a rule the maker should follow.

## Craft

- [ ] Concrete specifics appear throughout — real scent notes, real material names, real customer behaviors, real product sizes, real ranges. Not vague placeholders.
- [ ] Vocabulary specific to the category is captured (genre-specific terms, naming strategies, common claims, conventional ingredient/material language).
- [ ] Price ranges (if included) are bands with positioning context, not single numbers.
- [ ] Variations are framed as starting suggestions, not as a fixed schema.
- [ ] What-customers-ask-before-buying is grounded in observable customer concern data (reviews, FAQ patterns, common complaints) — not in priors.

## Tone

- [ ] The voice is grounded and sensory, not corporate.
- [ ] No designer or engineer jargon the maker wouldn't recognize.
- [ ] Negative examples in "What to avoid" are specific to the category, not generic e-commerce advice.

## Final read

- [ ] Reading the file as a fresh person, would you have a clear sense of who's in this category, how they vary, what their customers care about, and what the AI should know when generating a storefront for one of them?
- [ ] Would a maker reading this file feel seen across the range of possible positionings, or pigeonholed into one?

If every box is checked, the file is ready for human review.
