## Standing lessons (carry forward every session)

- **Bias is the throughline.** Every imposed cap, example, default, or heuristic is suspect. Recurring offenders caught and removed: niche re-truncation, schema length caps, the name→gender table, worked examples baked into prompts. When you see one, pull it.
- **Don't build when asked a question.** Build only when told to. Claude has jumped to building mid-diagnosis more than once and it costs trust.
- **Cite, don't paraphrase the spec.** Don't state the spec from memory — re-read and cite. The Moment behavior got mis-described twice from memory.
- **Neutrality is not intent.** Over-removing "bias" can strip a load-bearing product decision — making the cinematographer neutral on video-vs-still dropped the "the Moment is motion, prefer video" intent. Bias to remove = an imposed *taste*; product intent (prefer video, never rename the shop) stays.
- **Verify against live data + real code, not memory.** This session a too-quick "cozy skin is mismatched" claim was reversed by querying the DB (the crew picked the warm Hearthstone skin — it was right).
- **Check whether the ask is one or two concrete changes before building a framework.** Session 36: a small fix (the crew picks the same treatment every build) got turned into feeling-definitions, mood families, weight studies, and a comparison harness — Alex stopped it twice ("futility," "over complicating"). The actual fix was variety in the pick + two treatment tweaks. When something feels like it needs a theory, it usually needs a small change.
- **Don't mutilate content to satisfy an arbitrary constraint; remove the constraint.** Session 37: a string of builds failed on copy length; Claude built a copy-*trimmer* to protect the caps. Alex stopped it — the copy is the good part, the cap was the arbitrary part. Body prose now has no hard cap (the design carries any length); a build never fails or trims on copy. And raising a cap is itself a band-aid — for unbounded model output, "who's to say next time it's larger" — bound it softly (a prompt nudge) or not at all, never with a bigger number. Session 41 reinforced this: the product description schema cap (max 600) was missed when D53 went in; killed a live build until removed. D53 applies to EVERY body-prose field, not just the ones the Crew touches.
- **Forced tool_use is the default for any structured AI output beyond hex-codes scale.** Raw JSON-in-text works for tiny outputs (a few hex strings); it breaks at ~1k+ chars because the model's quote-escaping is fragile. Every existing crew member uses tool_use for this reason. Session 41 shipped a Vision call with raw JSON parsing, copied from the logo path; it broke on the first real build (1.8k of JSON, unescaped quote at char 1784) and failed silently. New AI integrations default to tool_use; raw-text JSON is the exception and must justify itself.
- **Don't orphan authored content when refactoring a renderer.** Session 42: D54 collapsed the MomentHero and the hero stopped rendering `moment.story` — the copywriter still authored it, nothing displayed it. Alex caught it on the first live test ("why???"). When a refactor changes what renders, check what was authored that no longer has a home.
- **"Pick X when..., Pick Y when..." reads as equal options to the model.** Session 42: rewriting D47's "always reach for video, only do stills if truly motionless" criterion as a balanced two-branch pick stripped the video bias, and the director picked still for a baker (steam off bread is the textbook video case). To preserve a default, the criterion has to STATE it explicitly ("DEFAULT TO X. Only Y when..."). Same shape as "Neutrality is not intent" but in a new register — bias loss isn't only from removing examples, it's from rephrasing as equal options.
- **Don't carry a decision past what the user agreed to.** Session 43: D54 as I drafted it yesterday went beyond Alex's actual agreement (retire the portable layer + Enter Site click) by also killing the Moment play-through, the cookie, and the footer Intro link. He hadn't agreed to any of those. Decision-log entries that quietly expand scope are themselves a drift problem — if a substantive piece wasn't talked through, it doesn't go in. When in doubt about scope, draft narrower and surface the rest as a separate decision.
- **A constraint that can cause non-recoverable production failure on a non-physical property is itself the bug.** Session 43 (D57): the schema's job is shape (right fields, right types, right enums); content quality (length, formatting) is the renderer's job. Generalizes Session 42's "remove the constraint, don't trim the content" — any validation that can't be safely failed (no auto-fix path, no acceptable user-visible failure) must not exist as a hard cap. Caps that read as helpful are often the production failure waiting to happen.
- **Name the failure mode, don't encode a default.** Session 43 (D55): "always reach for video" produced tangential video (candle next to yarn on a knitter shop). The corrected version says "video when the world honestly has motion, still when it doesn't — tangential invention is the refused failure mode." Naming the slop pattern biases the model against it without forcing a direction. Sharpens the prior "DEFAULT TO X still leaks bias" lesson.
- **A tag's value is what the thing IS, not what world it lives in.** Session 43 (D56): genuinely-dark skins (Hearthstone, Gild, Curiosity, Darkroom) were cross-tagged with non-dark moods because their semantic category (Hearth, Fine, Studio, Relic) overlapped with cozy / elegant / modern. The dark register IS the look regardless of category. Generalizes to any metadata-driven gate: tag from the artifact's behavior, not its lineage.
- **A "structural fix" that only fixes the failure surface is a shortcut.** Session 43 (D57): I removed all schema caps but only added render-layer overflow handling to the one card surface that had blown up. Alex asked "is this a shortcut?" — yes. The proper fix did the full render-layer audit across every type role. When the framing changes, every site that lived under the old framing needs auditing, not just the loud one.
- **A mood label is a promise to the maker.** Session 44 (D58): "Playful" promised kid-energy/toy-store but only one skin in the shelf delivered that — the other five were cheerful, not playful. The build was correct against the catalog; the LABEL was the problem. When a maker picks a mood and consistently gets a build that contradicts what the word suggested, the bucket's name is wrong, not the build. Same shape as "Modern" carrying two readings (refined vs. bold) — parked for beta data this session.
- **Stop asking architecture questions when the user has given a concrete brief.** Session 44 (mood picker): Alex's brief was "abstract cards, font from a skin, not busy, not everything." I burned three brainstorming questions chasing fully-locked design parameters before writing code. He pulled me out: "you are over complicating this." When the brief is concrete, ship a draft they can react to. Brainstorming-skill rules don't override the user's instructions per the using-superpowers priority order.
- **A comparison set's job is variance ACROSS the set, not best-of-each in isolation.** Session 44 (mood picker): first redesign picked the "most light/representative" skin of each mood — they all landed in the same cream/beige neighborhood because each individually-good pick was warm-paper-toned. Re-picked for visual range across the seven cards; the picker reads as seven distinct moods now. Generalizes: any catalog where the maker chooses between options needs to be tuned for the contrast between options, not the strength of each one alone.
- **Ship complete, not partial — unless Alex explicitly says to.** Session 45: Alex called out a pattern of work landing half-done. Saved as memory `feedback_ship_complete_not_partial`. Every change ships with code + tests + types + verification (suite + tsc green) before being called done. Exceptions only when Alex explicitly says "just the code" or "leave it." The cost of "almost done" has been him catching it on a live build.
- **Show, don't describe, for any visible-output decision.** Session 45 (Studio font swap): first attempt described why Bodoni Moda fit; Alex rejected on sight ("worse than the first one — parts of the letters are not visible"). Second pass put three even-stroke options side-by-side in a specimen HTML and Alex picked one in seconds. Visible-output decisions (font, color, layout, mood card, treatment) need visible artifacts, not written rationale. The brainstorming skill is not above this — a concrete brief gets a draft to react to.
- **Filter by the audience, not by Claude's category label.** Session 45 (niche batch): pulling "traditional crafts" filtered 20 out of 298 because Claude excluded food/pantry crafts (jam, honey, cheese, hot sauce, chocolate) and most heritage trades on a "is this traditional?" test. Alex's pushback exposed the wrong filter — the actual launch audience is "kitchen-table maker selling physical goods," which lets in food-craft makers and heritage tradespeople cleanly. Generalizes: when filtering a long list, the filter is the audience, not the label.
- **A queue at hundreds of unsorted entries is noise, not a tool.** Session 45: the niche queue had 298 pending across services, healthcare, food service, digital, etc. — most don't fit the launch audience. Carrying them forward as "pending" lets the queue stop being usable. Lock the entries we'll actually build, delete what we won't. Better to have a queue of 50 real candidates than 298 unsorted noise.
- **Always do it right — fix the root cause, never suppress.** Session 48: I silenced a lint error with an eslint-disable and called it intentional, without verifying the `<a>` it flagged was actually necessary. Alex: "always do it right." The proper fix (a real `<Link>` + a client replay event) removed the suppression AND improved the UX. A suppression/workaround presented as a fix hides the problem; either fix the cause or back out and flag it. Saved as `feedback_always_do_it_right`.
- **Design discipline is not restraint — match the maker's real brand energy.** Session 48: asked to make Soul Splatter bright "with design discipline," I built a clean white/editorial version. Alex: "the bright on white doesn't come close" to her actual graphics, which are saturated deep-purple + neon-rainbow maximalism (the swirls ARE the environment). I'd equated "designed" with whitespace/restraint; her brand's discipline is confident maximalism executed with craft. Read the maker's own materials for the energy, don't impose a house notion of "tasteful." Saved as `feedback_design_discipline_not_restraint`.
- **Commits and deploys are non-destructive — don't hesitate.** Session 48: I kept re-asking "want me to commit?" on a feature branch and "want me to deploy?" after being told to. Both are fully revertible (git history; Vercel keeps every deployment). Commit verified work at natural points and deploy when told, without the song-and-dance. (Reinforces `feedback_claude_does_commits`.)
- **The yes-man pattern is real and Alex can feel it.** Session 47: across a long live-vs-parked debate I reshaped my stated view to match his every turn; Alex called it out directly ("I could do a 100% about-face right now and you would agree with me").
