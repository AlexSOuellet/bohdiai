# Craft-Fair and Market Vendors in the US: Who They Are, How They Get Paid, What They Need (2025-2026)

Research date: 2026-10-13. Note on sources: there is no recent, formal, national survey of craft-fair vendors. Most hard numbers come from (a) event-listing directories, (b) Etsy's seller census, (c) farmers-market academic/extension surveys, and (d) vendor-tool blogs that have a commercial interest. Reddit threads did not surface in the search index; Etsy's community forum ("Craft Fairs, it's a living" board) was the main source of vendor voice. Treat blog estimates as directional.

## Market size: events, vendors, revenue per show, booth fees, shows per year, seasonality

### Takeaway
There are tens of thousands of craft/market events a year (one directory alone lists ~30,500 for 2026) plus roughly 7,000-8,000 farmers markets. Median booth fees run about $165 at craft fairs and $30 at farmers markets; a typical vendor grosses a few hundred dollars on an average day and does somewhere between 6 and 20+ shows a year, peaking in November.

### Cited Findings
- TheCraftMap (a fair-listing directory) reports 30,509 fairs listed for 2026, peaking in November with 5,081 events. This is one site's listing count, not a census. — [TheCraftMap 2026 fairs](https://www.thecraftmap.com/fairs/2026) (via search snippet; page returned 429 on fetch, so full monthly breakdown not captured)
- No official USDA farmers-market total for 2025 was found. Directory is voluntary/self-reported; dataset last updated April 21, 2025. — [data.gov National Farmers Market Directory](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/national-farmers-market-directory)
- A third-party compilation from the USDA Local Food Portal lists ~7,056 US farmers markets (2026). — [Harvestly / DataHub](https://datahub.io/harvestlymarkets/us-farmers-markets-dataset). Older USDA figures: 7,864 (2012) and 8,268 at peak listing. — [USDA bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDAOC/bulletins/4d08e1)
- Canopy Season database snapshot (Sept 9, 2026; 9,593 published events, 972 with a verified booth fee; one season only): overall median booth fee $100; 25th pct $40 or under; 75th pct $257; top 10% start at $475. — [Canopy Season](https://canopyseason.com/blog/what-craft-fair-booth-fees-actually-cost/)
- Same source, median booth fee by event type: farmers market $30 (middle half $20-$55), holiday market $137.50 ($60-$279), festival $150 ($75-$300), craft fair $165 ($67.50-$355), artisan market $180 ($65-$350), county/state fair $267.50 ($100-$381). Application/jury fee median $30 (215 events). 109 events also take a commission, median 10% (range 3%-50%). — [Canopy Season](https://canopyseason.com/blog/what-craft-fair-booth-fees-actually-cost/)
- Pricing guide ranges: local fairs $50-150/day; juried and holiday markets $200-300+. — [Pandataps craft pricing guide](https://pandataps.com/craft-pricing/guides/craft-fair-pricing-guide)
- Sales per day estimates (vendor-tool blog): slow day $50-$200, average $300-$800, strong $800-$2,500; new vendors earn 30-50% less; top juried shows $3,000-$25,000 per weekend for established artists. — [VendorsMap](https://vendorsmap.com/blog/how-much-money-craft-fair)
- Alternative estimate: $200-$600 small local fair, $500-$1,500 medium weekend market, $1,000-$3,000+ large craft fair or holiday market. — [GetVendorLoop](https://getvendorloop.com/guides/how-craft-vendors-make-money)
- Break-even on a typical Saturday fair estimated at roughly $500-$800 revenue (VendorsMap); "6 to 12 well-chosen shows a year" for vendors who make fairs work. — [VendorsMap, Are craft fairs worth it](https://vendorsmap.com/blog/are-craft-fairs-worth-it)
- Part-time/hobby sellers do 1-2 shows per month; full-time craft businesses run weekly shows plus wholesale/online. — [GetVendorLoop](https://getvendorloop.com/guides/how-craft-vendors-make-money)
- Forum anecdotes: one seller averages 20 shows/year; another ~17/year, mostly multi-day juried. — [Etsy Community, How many shows do you do each year](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Craft-Fairs-it-s-a-living/How-many-shows-do-you-do-each-year/td-p/120177641)
- Rules of thumb for whether a show pays: "3x rule" (gross 3x booth fee) — [The Craft Map](https://www.thecraftmap.com/blog/craft-fair-booth-fees-guide); "7x rule" also circulates — [SmartAsset](https://smartasset.com/career/the-economics-of-craft-fairs)
- One organizer's fee history: moved from $65 table fees (50 vendors) to $200 booth fees (105 exhibitors); venue rental $2,000-$8,000. — [Craft Industry Alliance](https://craftindustryalliance.org/how-to-plan-an-indie-craft-fair/)
- Old baseline (2000 data, cited 2008): CODA estimated ~127,000 US craft artists; half sold direct to public, and half of those at craft fairs. — [Bay State Banner, 2008](https://baystatebanner.com/2008/11/19/working-the-craft-show-circuit-is-a-tough-lifestyle)
- Craft industry size $36B US (2018, Association for Creative Industries; this is mostly craft-supply consumer spending, not vendor sales). — cited via [Customcy](https://customcy.com/blog/craft-handmade-stats/). Customcy's own "$343B handmade market 2026" figure is unverifiable; do not use.
- Farmers market vendors (Minnesota FM360 2023 survey): markets supply on average 57.8% (median 60%) of vendor business revenue. — [FM360 2023 vendor survey](https://fm360.umn.edu/2023-vendor-survey-summary-fm360)

### Inferences
- With a $165 median craft-fair booth plus ~$30 jury fee and travel, a vendor needs several hundred dollars in sales just to break even. A $15/mo tool (= $180/yr) is roughly the cost of one median craft-fair booth, so its value story has to be "helps you drop or pick one show" or "captures sales you would have lost."
- Seasonality: November is the peak month by event count (directory data). Vendors' tool need is concentrated spring through December; January-March is light, which creates churn risk on a monthly plan and favors the 10x yearly plan being priced/marketed before the holiday season.
- Farmers-market vendors (median booth $30, weekly cadence) are a different, more frequent, lower-ticket customer than craft-fair makers; per-show profit tracking matters more for the craft-fair crowd where fees are 5x higher.

### Gaps
- No authoritative count of US craft fairs/shows per year or of active handmade vendors who sell in person. Directory counts overlap and are not censuses.
- No survey-based average sales per show; all figures above are blog estimates or anecdotes.
- Full monthly breakdown (Jan-Mar low point) from TheCraftMap could not be fetched (HTTP 429).
- No current USDA official farmers-market total for 2025.

## Payment mix at craft fairs and markets (2025-2026), trends and problems

### Takeaway
No measured payment-mix survey for craft fairs exists. The consensus from vendor guides and organizers is that cards are the dominant electronic method, Venmo and Cash App are now commonly expected, cash remains important at small/rural events, and some events are going cashless. The main problems are fees (Venmo goods-and-services 1.9% + $0.10), signal loss at outdoor events, and no clean record tying a peer-to-peer payment to a sale.

### Cited Findings
- "The most common electronic payment method at craft shows is via credit and debit cards. Also popular are apps such as Venmo and Cash App." — [Successful Craft Shows](https://successfulcraftshows.com/craft-show-payment-methods/)
- Payment-processor guide says Venmo has become one of the most popular methods at craft fairs and recommends cash, credit cards, Venmo and PayPal at minimum (commercial source). — [QuadraPay](https://quadrapay.com/accept-payments-vendor-fairs-craft-markets/)
- NC State Crafts Fair 2025: organizer went cashless for its own sales and "strongly encourage[s]" every vendor to take some electronic payment (PayPal, Square, Venmo, CashApp). — [NCSU 2025 Crafts Fair info sheet](https://crafts.arts.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/77/2025/04/2025-Crafts-Fair-Information-Sheet.pdf)
- Counterpoint: a small festival FAQ says cash is preferred by most vendors, with a few taking Venmo or card. — [Cornell project FAQ](https://pages.github.coecis.cornell.edu/info1300-2022fa/tsl54-project3/faqs.html) (single small event, 2022)
- Farmers markets: at markets accepting electronic payment, only 6% of sales were cash (Washington State University study; date not given, likely older). — [WSU News](https://archive.news.wsu.edu/?p=138639)
- Only 51% of US counties have at least one farmers market that accepts credit cards (USDA 2022). — [Journal of Small Business Strategy](https://jsbs.scholasticahq.com/article/129666-the-digital-pivot-exploring-credit-card-and-e-payment-utilization-in-direct-to-consumer-u-s-agriculture)
- Consumer trend: card transaction value forecast +4.3% in 2025 as cards keep taking share from cash and checks. — [eMarketer, State of Payment Methods 2025](https://www.emarketer.com/content/state-of-payment-methods-2025)
- Venmo fees: goods-and-services payments cost the seller 1.9% + $0.10, on a business profile or when a personal-account buyer tags the payment as goods/services; no monthly fee; fee not refunded on refunds; instant transfer 1.75% (min $0.25); Venmo tap-to-pay 2.29% + $0.10. — [PaymentCloud](https://paymentcloudinc.com/blog/venmo-business-fees/); [Wise](https://wise.com/us/blog/venmo-goods-and-services-fee); [Swipesum](https://www.swipesum.com/insights/venmo-for-business-fees-features-and-how-to-accept-payments)
- On a personal account the buyer chooses whether to mark the payment as goods/services, so many booth payments go through as "friends" payments with no fee (and no purchase protection). — [Wise](https://wise.com/us/blog/venmo-goods-and-services-fee)
- Square (after Oct 2025 restructure): in-person 2.6% + 15c; online/invoices 3.3% + 30c; free plan has no monthly fee. — [Square vs Shopify, First Pier](https://www.firstpier.com/resources/square-vs-shopify)
- Tax reporting: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025, retroactive to 2022) restored the Form 1099-K threshold to more than $20,000 AND more than 200 transactions, eliminating the planned $2,500 (2025) and $600 (2026) thresholds. IRS FAQ issued Oct 23, 2025 (IR-2025-107). Some states keep lower thresholds. — [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/blog/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html); [OnPay](https://onpay.com/insights/1099-reporting-threshold-updates/)
- Etsy's own in-person card reader draws complaints: declined cards at fairs, sales that failed to process during Etsy outages; some sellers keep show payments on a separate processor. — [Etsy Community, Payment Processing Problems](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Craft-Fairs-it-s-a-living/Payment-Processing-Problems/m-p/105499144)

### Inferences
- The realistic booth mix is "card via Square or similar + Venmo/Cash App + cash." No single tool records all of them; Square records card and its own cash entries, but Venmo/Cash App/Zelle payments land in separate apps with no item detail. That gap (one ledger across all payment types per show) is the clearest unmet need the product addresses.
- The 1099-K rollback to $20k/200 removes the 2024-2025 fear that pushed hobby sellers to move off personal Venmo; most small vendors will again fall under the reporting threshold, so "tax compliance" is a weaker selling point than it would have been under the $600 rule.

### Gaps
- No measured share of card vs cash vs P2P at US craft fairs in 2025-2026. Square/PayPal merchant reports with event-vendor breakdowns were not found.
- Zelle-specific usage at booths: no data found.
- No data quantifying "mismatched payment" problems (wrong amount sent, payment to wrong person); this is anecdotal at best.

## How vendors track show profitability today

### Takeaway
Tracking is mostly DIY: spreadsheets, notebooks, and the processor's sales report, with "was it worth it" judged by gut against booth fee. A small cottage market of single-purpose trackers already exists (free web tools, a $6.99 one-time app, Etsy-sold spreadsheet templates), which proves the need but sets a very low price anchor for tracking alone.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor forum math on whether a show paid: a $275-booth April show where the vendor wanted ~$500/day to call it worthwhile; another poster: "$700 after booth expenses is nothing to sneeze at." — [Etsy Community, Help with perspective](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Craft-Fairs-it-s-a-living/Help-with-perspective-please/td-p/37741571)
- A vendor spent over $500 in vendor fees and earned about half back, then quit all shows except big holiday ones; another netted $70 after a church show's 25% organizer fee. — [Etsy Community, Why can't I let it go](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Craft-Fairs-it-s-a-living/Why-can-t-I-let-it-go/td-p/102079297); [Etsy Community, Where are craft fairs still profitable](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Etsy-Success/Where-are-craft-fairs-still-profitable-to-participate-in/m-p/27048314/highlight/true)
- Vendors swap show-by-show reviews in regional forum threads (e.g., "Local Craft Show Reviews"). — [Etsy Community, Capital Craftfair Connection](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Capital-Craftfair-Connection/Local-Craft-Show-Reviews/td-p/30562286)
- Free craft-fair profitability tracker; creator found little year-to-year variation for most shows, so past results predict future ones. — [Maker's Business Toolkit](https://makersbusinesstoolkit.com/cfpt/)
- BoothTally: $6.99 one-time; rates each fair "Rebook" (cleared $150 net), "Watch fees," or "Skip." — [BoothTally](https://boothtally.com/)
- BoothKeeper: free tracker computing real hourly wage per show. — [BoothKeeper](https://www.boothkeeper.com/)
- Paid Google Sheets "Craft Fair Tracker / Vendor Sales & Profit Tracker" templates sold on Etsy. — [Etsy listing](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4530231022/craft-fair-tracker-spreadsheet-craft)
- Guides recommend one tab per event: booth fee, sales, product cost, travel/food, weather. — [The Craft Map, inventory management](https://www.thecraftmap.com/blog/craft-fair-inventory-management)
- A craft show evaluation template is published by woodturning blog (indicates long-standing demand). — [Turning for Profit](https://www.turningforprofit.com/craft-show-evaluation-template/)

### Inferences
- "Was this show worth it" is a real, recurring vendor question, and the tooling that answers it is either free or under $10 one-time. Per-market tracking cannot carry a $15/mo price by itself; it has to be bundled with things vendors already pay for (website, catalog, online checkout) or with the POS that captures the sales automatically.
- The automated advantage (sales by payment method flowing straight from the booth POS into the per-show P&L) is the differentiator over spreadsheets that require manual entry.

### Gaps
- No survey quantifying what share of vendors track per-show profit at all vs. not tracking.
- Reddit (r/craftfairs, r/EtsySellers) threads did not surface in the search index; vendor-voice evidence here is from Etsy forums only.

## Pain points at the booth

### Takeaway
The best-documented pain is connectivity: card readers fail at crowded outdoor events, and offline mode risks declined cards after the buyer walks away. Lost sales from cash-only booths, slow checkout, and inventory not syncing between booth and online shop are also cited. Theft and line management were not documented in sources found. Sales tax is a genuine multi-state headache with rules that vary by state.

### Cited Findings
- Readers built for steady wifi stall at outdoor events where cell coverage is spotty and crowds overload towers; data can slow by early afternoon. — [Kolton Consulting](https://kolton.consulting/resources/card-reader-loses-signal-farmers-market); [Home Business Magazine](https://homebusinessmag.com/sales/events/card-reader-connection-pop-up-markets/)
- Offline payment queuing risks a decline after the goods are handed over; pending transactions can be lost; readers have a short lifespan when unused; helpers need training on refunds/voids. — [Maker's Business Toolkit (July 2024, UK-oriented)](https://makersbusinesstoolkit.com/must-credit-card-reader-craft-fairs-one-choose/)
- Customers without cash leave to find an ATM and some never return; customers may assume a stall doesn't take cards. — [Maker's Business Toolkit](https://makersbusinesstoolkit.com/must-credit-card-reader-craft-fairs-one-choose/)
- An item sold at the booth can still show as in stock online because nothing synced the count. — [Kolton Consulting](https://kolton.consulting/resources/card-reader-loses-signal-farmers-market)
- Washington farmers-market study: running out of cash limits purchases. — [WSU News](https://archive.news.wsu.edu/?p=138639)
- Sales tax: most states require vendors to register/collect/remit; terminology varies ("temporary vendor," "occasional sales," "special event"); no state permit needed only in AK, DE, NH, MT, OR (AK and MT have local tax). Examples: Connecticut requires a permit even for one-day events, displayed at booth; Maryland 30-day temporary license; Kansas special-event return due within 25 days; Arkansas daily collection; Arizona promoters may run vendors under the promoter's license; Kentucky temporary vendors must file even if the organizer remits. — [Avalara state-by-state guide](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/learn/whitepapers/craft-fairs-and-sales-tax-a-state-by-state-guide.html); [TaxJar](https://taxjar.com/blog/services/festival-sales-tax); [Hands Off Sales Tax](https://handsoffsalestax.com/how-to-charge-sales-tax-at-craft-shows/)
- In-person sales charge the rate where the sale takes place. — [Hands Off Sales Tax](https://handsoffsalestax.com/how-to-charge-sales-tax-at-craft-shows/)

### Inferences
- A phone POS that keeps working offline and records cash/P2P sales locally addresses the connectivity pain directly for non-card payments; card payments still depend on the processor.
- Per-event sales tax rate (set per market) is a feature vendors who cross state lines would value, since rate and filing obligations follow the event location.
- One-of-a-kind items double-sold between the booth and an online shop is a plausible pain for makers with online catalogs; a single shared catalog/inventory is the fix, but evidence is thin.

### Gaps
- No sources found on theft at booths, line length/slow checkout quantified, or double-selling incidents specifically between Etsy and the booth.

## Willingness to pay for software and price sensitivity

### Takeaway
Small makers already pay a stack of per-sale fees (Etsy 6.5% + 3% + $0.25 + $0.20 listing; Square 2.6% + 15c in person; Venmo 1.9% + $0.10) and keep fixed subscriptions low (Square free plan, Shopify $5 Starter that lacks a real storefront; a real Shopify store is ~$29-39/mo). Competing per-show trackers are free to $6.99 one-time. Price sensitivity is high, but no direct survey evidence on "subscription fatigue" was found.

### Cited Findings
- Etsy: $0.20 per listing, 6.5% transaction fee, plus Etsy Payments processing 3% + $0.25. — [NerdWallet, Shopify vs Etsy](https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/learn/shopify-vs-etsy); [Global Sources](https://www.globalsources.com/knowledge/shopify-vs-etsy/)
- Shopify Starter $5/mo is a link-in-bio/social selling tool, not a full online store; a storefront with custom domain needs Basic (~$39/mo, ~$29 annual; one source lists $19 annual — sources conflict). — [DemandSage](https://www.demandsage.com/?p=34726); [NerdWallet Shopify pricing](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/small-business/shopify-pricing)
- Shopify app add-ons (email, reviews, inventory, analytics) typically cost $20-$100/mo each. — [GetOneCart](https://www.getonecart.com/shopify-fees/)
- Square free plan: no monthly fee; 2.6% + 15c in person, 3.3% + 30c online (post-Oct 2025). — [First Pier](https://www.firstpier.com/resources/square-vs-shopify)
- Venmo Business: no setup or monthly fees; 1.9% + $0.10 per transaction. — [PaymentCloud](https://paymentcloudinc.com/blog/venmo-business-fees/)
- Single-purpose show trackers: BoothTally $6.99 one-time; BoothKeeper free; Maker's Business Toolkit free. — [BoothTally](https://boothtally.com/); [BoothKeeper](https://www.boothkeeper.com/); [Maker's Business Toolkit](https://makersbusinesstoolkit.com/cfpt/)
- Etsy 2024 census (via third-party summary): creative business contributes on average 11% of household income — i.e., most sellers are earning supplemental income. — [Patreon summary of Etsy 2024 census](https://www.patreon.com/posts/125639678); [LinkMyBooks](https://linkmybooks.com/blog/how-many-sellers-are-on-etsy)

### Inferences
- $15/mo ($150/yr on the yearly plan) sits above the free/one-time tools and Square's $0 plan but well below a working Shopify Basic store plus apps. The pitch that holds is "replaces a website + catalog + a tracker" rather than "a POS," because the POS slot is free from Square/Venmo.
- $25/mo with online checkout is in Shopify Basic (annual) territory; it competes on simplicity and per-show tracking, not price.
- For sellers earning ~11% of household income from the craft business, $180-300/yr is meaningful; a yearly plan paid once before the holiday season is likely easier to accept than a recurring monthly charge through slow January-March.

### Gaps
- No survey or quantified evidence on subscription fatigue among hobby sellers; Reddit price-complaint threads not surfaced.
- No data on what share of craft vendors pay for any website/store subscription at all.

## Demographics of craft vendors

### Takeaway
The best proxy is Etsy's seller census: about 80% women, average age ~43 (2024), roughly a quarter rural, nearly all one-person home businesses, mostly supplemental income though ~70% call it a business. Off-platform selling (incl. craft fairs) is common: 56% sold through other channels in 2021.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 Etsy census (third-party breakdown, author flags some unclear wording): ~80% women, ~1 in 4 rural, average age 43, ~5.6M sellers at end of 2024. — [Patreon summary](https://www.patreon.com/posts/125639678)
- 2024: 70% consider their shop a business; 29% say their creative business is their sole occupation; it contributes 11% of household income on average; 89% are one-person businesses and 97% operate from home (Ipsos survey, 2,402 sellers, six markets). — [LinkMyBooks](https://linkmybooks.com/blog/how-many-sellers-are-on-etsy)
- 2021: 56% sold through other channels too (other platforms, social, craft fairs, retail); Etsy was the largest channel for multi-channel sellers. — [EcommerceBytes, 2022](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2022/03/31/the-sellers-who-sold-12-billion-on-etsy-in-2021/)
- 2017 US: 87% women vs 33% of US business owners; median age 37 vs ~50 for business owners; 28% rural. — [Etsy US 2017 Seller Census](https://extfiles.etsy.com/advocacy/Etsy_US_2017_SellerCensus.pdf) (via search summary)
- 2015 US: median age 39, 86% women, 56% college degree, median household income $56,180. — [Thryv summary of Etsy report](https://www.thryv.com/blog/new-etsy-report-reveals-emergence-creative-entrepreneur/)
- Farmers market vendors (MN 2023): markets are ~58% of their business revenue on average. — [FM360](https://fm360.umn.edu/2023-vendor-survey-summary-fm360)

### Inferences
- The Etsy seller base is aging (median 37 in 2017 to average 43 in 2024) and in-person craft-fair vendors are likely older still than online-only sellers (forum voice and show-circuit culture suggest 40s-60s), implying moderate tech comfort: phones yes, complex setup no. A done-for-you setup model fits this audience.
- Women-majority, one-person, home-based, supplemental-income profile reinforces price sensitivity and the need for very simple booth tools.

### Gaps
- No direct demographic survey of in-person craft-fair vendors (age, tech comfort, hobby vs business) found; Etsy data is a proxy and is online-weighted.
- Etsy's own 2024 census PDF was not retrieved; 2024 figures come from secondary summaries.
